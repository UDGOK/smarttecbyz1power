# Simulated investor review — 11 September 2026

Three independent AI reviewers examined the existing model and presentation from commercial, operations and investment-return perspectives. Each raised five questions; a second round evaluated the planned revisions. This is a consolidated record of their questions and the evidence-based answers, not a transcript or feedback from actual investors. No investors were contacted and no investment commitments were received.

## Commercial perspective

| Question | Current answer and resulting revision |
| --- | --- |
| What exactly does a customer buy? | Proposed dedicated B300 capacity. Workload, storage, networking, security and support scope remain to be agreed. Added a customer-offer slide rather than implying a complete priced service. |
| Is $6.50 contracted? | It is the owner's proposed GPU-hour price. No signed customer pricing was supplied. The deck identifies it as an assumption. |
| How much paid demand is needed? | The illustrated 60-saleable-GPU case assumes 1,200 paid GPU-hours daily from month 4: 20 hours per GPU, or 83.3% of saleable capacity. This is a target, not bookings. |
| What supports collection and retention? | Customer credit, enforceable minimum payments, cancellation terms and paid workload validation require evidence. The 98% collection and 10% selling-fee inputs remain assumptions. |
| Why should global AI demand translate into SmartTec revenue? | It does not establish local bookings. The presentation separates the global IEA market backdrop from the customer commitments needed before procurement. |

## Operations perspective

| Question | Current answer and resulting revision |
| --- | --- |
| What power is actually reserved for SmartTec? | Management reports a solar/battery/gas agreement below $0.07/kWh. Exclusive continuous kW, operating date and full cost inclusions remain pending the team. The deck retains this open requirement. |

## Return and capital perspective

| Question | Current answer and resulting revision |
| --- | --- |
| Does positive monthly cash mean an attractive investor return? | No. The 60+4 illustration has about $6.95m total contributions, $1.99m five-year net cash profit, 28.7% total five-year ROI and approximately negative $674,000 NPV at an assumed 15% annual discount rate. All remain visible. |
| When is the original capital recovered? | Modeled project payback is month 49. Added a cumulative distributions-minus-contributions chart, including initial capital, later calls and terminal reserve release. |
| Why is more capital called after distributions? | The month-37 $536,000 overhaul produces an approximately $107,000 call under the existing reserve/distribution rules. A different reserve policy needs a separate model and agreement; no economic inputs were silently changed. |
| Does the smallest positive-NPV fleet solve the problem? | The tested 72-GPU/no-reserve case has only $7,361 positive NPV and fails downside cases. It is not presented as a robust recommendation. Higher contracted price or lower procurement cost still needs downside cushion. |
| What investment is actually being offered? | Final raise, valuation, security, equity rights and waterfall are undecided. Added an early investment-decision brief and a closing invitation to diligence. The 15% discount rate is not an offered investor return. |

## Second-round conclusion and implemented changes

All three reviewers remained at **diligence only**, rather than a capital commitment. They considered the proposed revisions useful for resolving presentation gaps, while identifying substantive evidence gaps that artwork cannot solve.

The revised 26-page presentation adds an early funding/decision brief, a concrete customer offer, visual GPU allocation, cumulative capital recovery and proposed diligence responsibilities. It ends with a specific investment discussion invitation. Three original Runway concept images are embedded in the PDF and investor page, labeled to distinguish them from installed assets. Financial assumptions were preserved.

Before procurement, the remaining requirements are enforceable customer payments and fully costed service economics; a reviewed power agreement and accepted engineering design; complete OEM/operating quotations; and agreed investor rights, reserves and capital-release conditions. The earlier two-RTX/one-B300 physical starter concept is distinguished from the 60+4 financial alternative.

Review outcomes do not predict investment conversion. The next useful step is to assemble the missing evidence and rerun the model, rather than increase fleet size to manufacture an attractive return.
